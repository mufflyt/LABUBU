2. Power Analysis: Have We Met 80% Power?

Direct Answer:
  • For subtle identity gaps (Odds Ratios OR 1.5 to 2.0): No. Achieved power ranges from 5.9% to 28.2%.
  • For large structural policy gaps (OR ≥ 3.3): Yes. The Straight vs. Single Mother contrast achieves 81.6% power at OR = 3.3 and 93.3% power at OR = 4.6.

In a within-practice paired audit study, statistical power is governed strictly by the number of discordant practices (practices that treat two scenarios differently). Because 69% to 80% of practices treat all callers identically (high baseline concordance), discordant pairs are relatively rare:

Contrast Paired Practices (n) Discordant Practices (nd) Power at OR = 1.5 Power at OR = 2.0 MDE at 80% Power (α=0.05)
Straight vs. Single Mother 54 17 (11 vs 6) 12.9% 28.2% OR = 3.3 (81.6% power)

To achieve 80% power for a modest odds ratio of OR = 2.0 (a ~10–12 percentage point gap), we would require 72 discordant practices (approximately 218 complete paired practices).

Wait-Time Power: Only 8 to 10 practices have observed appointment dates across paired scenarios. Power to detect a 5-business-day wait difference is <8%; minimum detectable wait differences at 80% power remain 26 to 33 business days. Wait times must be reported as exploratory / descriptive only.

3. How LABUBU Compares to Literature Benchmarks

Audit studies in published medical and health services literature fall into two distinct tiers:

  • Structural / Insurance Audit Studies (e.g. NEJM, JAMA, Annals): Focus on large policy barriers (such as Medicaid vs. Private Insurance) where effect sizes are massive (OR ≥ 3.0 to 10.0+; 20–50% gaps). In this category, 30 to 60 paired practices are standard. LABUBU matches literature requirements for detecting large structural barriers.
  • Identity & Demographic Audit Studies (e.g. American Sociological Review, Health Affairs): Focus on subtle discrimination by race, sexual orientation, or family structure (OR ≈ 1.4 to 1.8; 5–15% gaps). These studies require 200 to 350 paired practices (70–100 discordant pairs) to achieve 80% power. LABUBU (48 triads) is sized as a high-precision feasibility / regional audit.

  3. Highlight high concordance as a positive finding: Most RRM practices in this sample evaluate callers on clinical/insurance criteria rather than caller family structure.
